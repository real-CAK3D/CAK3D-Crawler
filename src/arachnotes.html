Arachnotes turns anything you read into notes you can study and cite. You give it a text, and a spider crawls it one keyword at a time, the way a web crawler follows links across the internet. Every keyword it catches is sorted into a topic, tied to the sentences it came from, and spun into a web you can explore. This page is a real crawl: watch the spider work through it while you read.

Open almost anything

Paste text, or open a link. Wikipedia articles come in clean, with their pictures, captions and licenses, and other sites are read through a reader service so menus and ads are left behind. You can search the web from the app and crawl several pages together as one text.

Files work too: a PDF keeps its page numbers for citations, an EPUB book opens chapter by chapter, and a Word document, a web page you saved, a spreadsheet, subtitles, a notebook or a Markdown note opens as plain reading text. A photo of a page or a scanned PDF is read on your device with optical character recognition, so the picture never leaves your phone. One text can be up to about 600,000 characters, the length of a long novel.

Try it on the real internet

Keywords and topics

The spider doesn't just count words. It reads each keyword in context, looks at which keywords turn up in the same sentences, a measure called co-occurrence, and decides which topic the keyword belongs to. It's a small topic model that runs entirely on your device, with no internet needed.

Open the live stats to see every decision as it's made, and how sure the spider is. Keywords it isn't sure about come to you to settle, or to your AI if you've set one up. Type a focus topic and the spider hunts the matching keywords first, as if they were prey. Select any words on the page to add your own keyword, and click a keyword to see its topic, its sentences and your notes.

The web, the nest and the graph

Tap Web to see everything the spider found as a real orb web: the main subject sits at the hub, each topic hangs on a spoke, and tapping a topic opens a new web of its own keywords. Tap again for the notes on that keyword, with every sentence it came from.

The Nest lays the same keywords out as a force-directed graph you can zoom into and drag around; the positions you choose are saved. The Graph tab turns the crawl into bar charts of topics and keywords, the Timeline shows where each topic turns up from the start of the text to the end, and the Media tab gathers the pictures and videos with their credits and Creative Commons licenses.

Study with it

As it goes, the spider wraps what it has caught into egg sacs, and opening a sac shows a summary of those keywords with their sources. Settings can sort the sacs into one per topic instead. The study guide pulls the key points, terms and questions out of the whole text, and saves as an outline with the pictures and their citations.

Flashcards are made from your keywords automatically and scheduled with spaced repetition, in the spirit of the Leitner system: cards you know come back less often, and cards you miss come back soon. Testing yourself like this is called the testing effect, and it is one of the best-studied ways to remember. The idea goes back to Hermann Ebbinghaus, who described the forgetting curve in 1885. The app can also read any passage aloud with speech synthesis.

Cite as you go

Every keyword, note and answer points back to the sentence it came from, numbered by source and page. Choose APA, MLA or Chicago style and the bibliography follows. Pictures are cited too, with their author and license. Export your sources to Zotero or BibTeX, or share the whole crawl as a single web page.

Ask the text

Type a question and Arachnotes finds the passages that answer it, with their citations, using information retrieval on your device. If you connect an AI with your own key, a large language model also writes a short answer that cites those passages. The key stays on your device, and you can use a free model on your own computer instead.

Read code

Pages → Read code opens a separate code reader. Paste code, open files or a whole project as a .zip, or give it a GitHub link. It maps every function, class and variable as a web, shows where each one is defined, read, changed and called, follows the calls between them like a call graph, and explains the whole codebase in plain words. It reads JavaScript, Python, Java, C, C++, C#, Go, Rust, Kotlin, Swift, PHP, Ruby, Dart and more.

Try it on real code

Make it yours

Choose the kind of spider, its look, its web style and how it hunts, then pick a theme, weather and sounds. Your crawls stay private: there are no accounts, and everything stays on your device and works offline once it's open. Save crawls in the library, move them to another device with a weave code or a crawl file, and keep a backup of everything. In Settings → Research, choose whether the app opens on this guide, on what you were reading last, or on a blank page.

A quick how-to

  • Open Pages → Open text, and paste text, pick a file, or enter a link.
  • Watch the spider crawl, or tap the page to send it somewhere.
  • Tap a keyword to see its topic, sentences and notes.
  • Tap Web to explore the topics, then the Nest and the Graph.
  • Open the study guide and flashcards when the crawl is done.
  • Copy a citation, or export your sources for your paper.
  • Tap ? and Point at anything whenever something is new.

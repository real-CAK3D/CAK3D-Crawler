Arachnotes is made by CAK3D_Creations. This policy covers the Arachnotes app for Android, the Arachnotes web app, and this website. The short version: there are no accounts, no ads and no tracking, and what you crawl stays on your device. The only things we ever receive are a random support ID, the issue reports you choose to send, and anything you post in the public forum.

What stays on your device

  • The texts, PDFs, books and photos you open, and everything the app makes from them: keywords, topics, egg sacs, notes, flashcards and study progress.
  • Your library of saved crawls and your settings, including what the app learns about your interests.
  • Your AI settings, including any API key you enter. The key is only sent to the AI service you chose.
  • Reading text out loud uses your device's built-in voices.
  • Reading the text in scanned pages and photos (OCR) happens on your device. Images are not uploaded.

This information is kept in your device's app storage. Uninstalling the app, or clearing the web app's data in your browser, deletes it. We never receive a copy.

When the app connects to other services

Some features need the internet. They only run when you use them, and they send only what that feature needs:

  • Opening a web page or crawling a site: the address you enter is fetched from Wikipedia (for Wikipedia pages) or through the Jina reader service (r.jina.ai) for other sites.
  • Searching the web: your search words are sent to DuckDuckGo through the Jina reader service, or to Wikipedia's search.
  • Summaries, answers and study guides from an AI: the passages being summarized and your question are sent to the AI service you set up in Settings, using your own API key, under that service's own privacy policy. That can be Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral, DeepSeek, xAI or Together AI. When you choose Load models, your key is sent to that service to list the models it can use. If you don't set up an AI, nothing is sent.
  • An AI on your own computer (Ollama or LM Studio): requests go over your home network to your computer and nowhere else. Find my computer looks for it by sending small requests to addresses on your home network.
  • Pictures and videos from pages: when you crawl a web page, the app keeps the addresses of its pictures and videos (with their captions and credits) and shows them from the site they came from, so that site sees your device's internet address when they load. For Wikipedia pages it also asks Wikimedia for each picture's author and license. Videos open on their own site (for example YouTube) when you choose to play them.
  • Sharing to Arachnotes: when you share a link, text or file to Arachnotes from another app, it goes straight into the app on your device, the same as opening it yourself.
  • Reading code from GitHub: only if you open a GitHub link in the code reader. The app asks GitHub for that project's list of files and downloads the code files. Code you paste or open from your device stays on your device.
  • Looking up keywords: unless you turn it off (Settings → Research → Look up keywords online), the app asks Wikipedia for a short summary of a keyword, or Wiktionary for a definition. That happens when you open a keyword, a flashcard, the study guide or the list of topics (which also asks Wikipedia for related reading), or, if you choose, as the spider catches each keyword. What's sent is the keyword, sometimes with a couple of keywords near it to find the right article, or an article title to find related ones. Never your text. Like any website, Wikipedia sees your device's internet address. What comes back is kept with the crawl so it works offline.
  • The free on-device AI: only if you turn it on in Settings. The app downloads a small open AI model from Hugging Face (huggingface.co) and the engine that runs it from jsDelivr, once, and keeps them on your device. After that it runs entirely on your device: what you read and ask is never sent anywhere. If your browser has its own built-in AI, that is used instead and nothing downloads.
  • Fonts and reading tools: the app may download fonts from Google Fonts, and the language data used for OCR from jsDelivr, the first time they're needed.

These services receive your device's internet address, as with any web request, and handle it under their own privacy policies.

Your support ID, issue reports and fixes

The first time you open the app, it makes a random support ID (it looks like AN-7K3Q-9XWD-2M4P) and keeps it on your device. It isn't based on your name, your Google account or anything about your device. You can see it in Settings → Help & support.

  • Checking for fixes: when the app starts, it sends your support ID to our support server to ask whether we've left a fix for your app (for example, resetting a setting that went wrong, or restoring an item you bought). Nothing else is sent, and these checks aren't stored.
  • Error reports: if something goes wrong in the app, it asks whether to send an error report, or sends one automatically if you chose "Always send" (you can change that in Settings → Help & support). A report contains the error message and where in the app it happened, the app version and platform, and your support ID. It never includes your texts, notes, library or API keys. Error reports are deleted after 90 days.
  • Emailing us: if you email arachnotes-app@yahoo.com, your message (who it is from, the subject and the text) is copied into our support database so we can read and answer it, and deleted from there after 90 days. Your email stays in our Yahoo mailbox as with any email.
  • Daily check-in: once a day, the app sends your support ID, the app version, and whether it is the Android or web version, so we can count how many people use Arachnotes and which versions are in use. Nothing else is sent: not your texts, notes or settings. Check-ins are deleted after about 13 months. Turn it off in Settings → Help & support ("Count this app in the number of people using Arachnotes").
  • Notifications: if you turn on "Notify me when a crawl finishes" (Settings → Research), the app shows a notification on your device when a crawl ends while it is in the background. The notification is made on your device; nothing is sent to us or anyone else to show it.
  • Reporting an issue: only if you press Send report in Settings → Help & support. The report contains what you typed, your support ID, the app version and platform, and, if you leave the box ticked, your app settings, the store items you own, your screen size and your browser or device name. It also contains your email address if you choose to give one so we can reply. Reports never include your texts, notes, library or API keys.

Reports are stored with our database provider, Supabase, on servers in the United States, and only we can read them. We use them only to fix problems and reply to you, and we delete them within 12 months, or sooner if you ask. To have your reports deleted, use the Delete your data page with your support ID; they are deleted straight away.

This website and its Issues and Ideas forum

  • No cookies, analytics or ads. The website is hosted by Vercel, which keeps short-lived server logs (such as your internet address and the page requested) to run and protect the service. Fonts load from Google Fonts. The download button asks GitHub for the latest version number.
  • Forum posts are public. Anything you post in Issues or Ideas, including the name you give (or "Anonymous"), can be read by anyone. Don't include personal details, your support ID or anything private; use Report an issue in the app for that.
  • Spam limits. When you post, reply, vote or report a post, our database stores a one-way scrambled version (a hash) of your internet address, so we can limit how much one place can post. We can't turn it back into your address.
  • Your browser keeps a random voting ID and whether you turned the spider off, in its own storage on your device. Clearing your browser's site data removes them.
  • Moderation. Posts that several people report are hidden automatically. We may hide or remove posts that break the forum rules. To have a post of yours removed, list it on the Delete your data page.

Phone & web: link codes, moving data, and the web version's free uses

  • The web version's free uses: without a link code, the web version at arachnotes.vercel.app can crawl 2 of your own uploads. To count them, your browser keeps the count, and our database keeps a one-way scrambled version (a hash) of your internet address with the number used. We never store the address itself. The count is deleted after a year without use.
  • Link codes: when you press Make a link code in the Android app, the app asks Google Play to confirm that it's the real Arachnotes installed from Google Play (Google's Play Integrity service), and sends that confirmation, your support ID and the purchase tokens Google Play gave the app to our server. The server checks them with Google and stores, in scrambled form, the code (for 30 minutes, and it works once) and the list of items you own. A browser that enters the code gets a random pass, kept in that browser and stored scrambled on our server with your owned items and support ID, so the web version has no limit there and your purchases are unlocked. A pass is deleted after a year without use, when you press Unlink this browser (it's removed from that browser), or when you ask us to delete your data. We don't receive your name, email, Google account or payment details.
  • Moving data between devices: when you make a transfer code in Settings → Phone & web, the crawls, notes and settings you chose are locked (encrypted with AES-256) on your device with a key made from the code, before anything is uploaded. Our server only ever holds the locked data, which it can't read, filed under a fingerprint of the code, with a scrambled version of your internet address to limit how many codes one place makes. It's deleted as soon as the other device picks it up, or after 24 hours if it doesn't.

Purchases

Paid packs are bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment and tells the app which packs your Google account owns. We don't see or store your payment details. If you link a browser with a link code, our server checks your purchases with Google as described above.

What we don't do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No collection of your texts, notes, library, contacts, location or files.
  • No analytics, advertising or tracking.
  • We don't sell or share your information.

Children

Arachnotes is not directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect information from them. Children under 13 shouldn't post in the forum.
